# Public-use rights review

Checked October 1, 2026, for a public Vercel experience linked from Hijinx and a possible future Substack article. This is a source and permission review, not legal clearance. No rights holder has been contacted and no license has been purchased. Existing artwork choices are retained locally.

## Practical conclusion

Do not describe the whole experience as rights-cleared. There are documented routes for older artworks, but the two O'Keeffe works need a specific public-use decision. ## Literary text

The current ten excerpts total about 463 words by a word-token count, including the two 73-word closing/gallery passages. This number is an inventory, not a legal threshold. The original text remains unchanged; the optional Pippa cut and closing trim have not been approved explicitly. Character labels identify the fictional speaker/narrator; Donna Tartt remains the author of all literary quotations.

The [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/more-info.html) identifies criticism and commentary as possible fair uses. It weighs purpose, the nature of the work, amount/substantiality, and market effect. There is no safe word count, and noncommercial use is not automatically fair. A novel's creative nature and use of memorable passages also matter. Lucie's passage-specific reflections give a concrete commentary purpose, but they do not establish permission or guarantee a fair-use outcome.

[Hachette's permissions FAQ](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/about/faqs/) says uses that qualify as fair use need no permission from them; otherwise its form routes requests, fees apply, and responses can take weeks. For a permission request, include all ten excerpts, edition/ISBN, exact display context, screenshots, a worldwide public website, its portfolio connection, and potential Substack use. A legal opinion on the specific design is an alternative to permission. No request has been submitted.

## O'Keeffe conditions that affect this design

The [museum's reproduction policy](https://www.okeeffemuseum.org/rights-and-reproductions/) limits alteration, cropping, overlays and animation, and asks for more complete adjacent credit information than the current title/artist labels. Access O'Keeffe's download terms allow certain educational/noncommercial uses but direct commercial/promotional use to the relevant rights holder. These policies do not decide the legal boundaries of fair use.

Our paintings retain their proportions, but the gallery pans past the viewport edge, grades the surrounding light and includes a moving bird. Confirm that the actual screen treatment is acceptable in a license request. A portfolio promotes Lucie's creative practice even if the experience has no sales or ads, so do not assume the noncommercial conditions apply. ARS licensing is not a dependable overnight route; the museum quotes a minimum of ten business days.
